### Overview
As we learn a new skill explicitly (e.g., learning how to play the piano), we first need to engage a lot of cognitive control to focus our attention on the relevant information and actions. However, as proficiency increases, cognitive control needs to be disengaged in order to automatise the newly learned skill. 

In adults, cognitive control disengagement with learning is evidenced by two changes in the EEG data: (1) a progressive reduction in frontal theta power across blocks of trials (slow timescale), and (2) a reduction in alpha power suppression across trials within each block (fast timescale). As children do not engage cognitive control as flexibly as adults, they may not show as efficient disengagement of cognitive control, potentially hampering automatization of the newly learned skills.
