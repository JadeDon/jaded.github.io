### Overview
While Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is clinically defined by social communication differences and restricted, repetitive behaviours, cognitive abilities across the spectrum remain highly heterogeneous. By re-analysing the data of a comprehensive battery of tasks, this study investigated how sub-domains of spatial cognition and language differentiate autistic and neurotypical individuals using data-driven Elastic Net regression modelling. Identified spatial verbal memory as a key differentiating predictor and explored cross-system cognitive integration differences in ASD.

### Core Findings
Both spatial cognition and language contribute to differentiating autism versus NT status. 

Spatial verbal memory is the strongest differentiating factor. The most differentiating skills often involve later-developing capacities or those that rely on efficient cross-system integration.
